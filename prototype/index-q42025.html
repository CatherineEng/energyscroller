One Nation, One Day of Energy

Have you ever stopped to wonder how much energy we use as a nation, in one day?

Take a wild guess.

11,400,000,000

kilowatt hours

That's 11.4 billion kWh!

To understand how much this is, we first have to know something about kilowatt hours.

Energy is measured in kilowatt hours, or kWh.
1 kWh = 1000 watts used in one hour. 

A page of 11.4 billion of these would take you about 50 days of scrolling fast to reach the end!

What can you do with 1 kWh anyway?

Lots of things...

You could watch YouTube for 24 hours straight.

Bake 3 batches of cookies in the oven.

Drive an electric vehicle for 3 miles.

Electric vehicles and transit systems already use 20 million kilowatt-hours every day. As more buses, trains, and cars go electric, this number will grow.

Choosing clean transportation today helps shape a future where travel is both efficient and powered by renewable energy.

America's colleges and universities are giant energy users, with labs, dorms, and classrooms running day and night.

Students have power beyond the classroom: small actions like switching off unused projectors, lights, and devices adds up to massive energy savings campus-wide.

Hospitals never sleep, and their lifesaving work depends on steady electricity for lights, machines, and cooling. Healthcare uses about 150 million kilowatt-hours a day.

Smarter systems and greener buildings can help cut waste without ever risking patient care.

Artificial intelligence now uses about 200 million kilowatt-hours every day - roughly 1/3 of all data center power.

Training big models is energy-hungry, but smarter chips and renewable power can shrink AI's growing footprint.

Every email, video, and search runs through a data center. Together, they use 400 million kilowatt-hours daily, in addition to the 200 million kilowatt-hours used by AI datacenters.

Cleaner energy and efficiency upgrades are key to powering our digital lives responsibly.

Schools use over 210 million kilowatt-hours daily. Hallway lights, computers, and heating all add up.

Students can take the lead - turning off classroom lights, unplugging chargers, and inspiring whole schools to save energy.

From courthouses to military bases, government facilities use a quarter-billion kilowatt-hours every day.

Federal and state agencies can set an example by cutting waste and switching to clean energy first.

Shopping centers are bright, cool, and open long hours—using 300 million kilowatt-hours daily.

Smarter lighting, efficient cooling, and mindful shoppers all help reduce the hidden cost of keeping stores running.

Warehouses keep America's goods moving, but all that lighting, refrigeration, and conveyor power uses 350 million kilowatt-hours daily.

Energy-efficient systems can deliver savings while keeping shelves stocked.

Big-box stores, grocery chains, and corner shops together use 550 million kilowatt-hours daily.

Better equipment and greener buildings help retailers save energy and money.

Offices account for 620 million kilowatt-hours daily—often with empty rooms running HVAC and lights.

Smarter building controls and employee choices can shrink this huge footprint.

Hotels and restaurants use 650 million kilowatt-hours daily.

Green equipment and small changes—like efficient lighting—can make hospitality more sustainable.

Churches, laundromats, gyms, banks, barbershops, libraries, and more fall into this group—nearly a billion kilowatt-hours daily.

Millions of small service spaces add up.

Factories and industry use ~2.87 billion kilowatt-hours every day in the U.S. Efficiency gains and offshoring kept this stable.

Industry remains one of our biggest energy users.

Homes use more energy than any other sector. The good news? Small choices—like switching off lights—add up.
